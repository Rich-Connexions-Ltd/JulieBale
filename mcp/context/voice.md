# Voice

How Julie Bale sounds. Apply this to every word that goes on the site.

## Character
Warm, conversational, experienced and lightly playful. Julie sounds like she is
talking to someone across the piano, not selling to them. Confident and human.

## Rules
- **British English** throughout (colour, realise, programme, recognise).
- **No em dashes.** Use full stops, commas, or "and". (Rewrite dashes out.)
- **Singing is always the primary subject.** Confidence and personal change are
  outcomes of singing, not generic coaching promises.
- Use **contractions** naturally (you're, it's, don't). Allow ordinary-life humour.
- Do **not** imply Julie was told she couldn't sing. She works with people who
  were told that.
- Use the signature line **"You just need to sing the first note"** sparingly,
  not on every page.
- Do not over-repeat **"35+ years"**. Once per page at most.
- Avoid generic marketing language: no "unlock your potential", no
  "transformational journey", no "elevate", no "empower".
- Keep paragraphs short. Let big statements be big and the copy around them brief.

## Names to keep exact
- Positioning: **Vocal Transformation Specialist & Mentor** (and dramatic soprano).
- Flagship: **From First Note to Final Curtain** (never reworded).
- Podcast: **What's Your Singing Story?** (exact).
- Brands: **Diva Energy®**, **Diva Framework®**, **Hope Sings Eternal**.

## The test
Does this feel like Julie Bale, an established classical artist and premium
mentor, rather than an online course business? If not, simplify.
