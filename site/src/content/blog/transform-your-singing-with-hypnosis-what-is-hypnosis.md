When I tell people that I use clinical hypnotherapy in my work with singers, the reactions fall into two camps. Either they've already experienced hypnotherapy and they get it immediately, or their frame of reference is stage hypnosis and they look at me with polite scepticism.

Let me start at the beginning, because understanding what hypnosis actually is makes everything else make sense.

**Hypnosis is a natural state.** The deeply focused, relaxed, highly receptive state that we call hypnosis is not an artificially induced trance. It is a natural human mental state that every person experiences multiple times a day — in the moments between sleeping and waking, in deep creative absorption, in the "highway hypnosis" of a long familiar drive. What formal hypnotherapy does is teach you to enter this state deliberately and use it for a specific purpose.

**Hypnosis bypasses the critical faculty.** The conscious mind — the part of you that analyses, judges, and argues — is a powerful governor. It is also, frequently, the source of the problem. The singer who consciously knows they can sing, yet feels terror in their body when they try to perform, is experiencing the disconnect between the conscious and the subconscious. Hypnotherapy bypasses the conscious filter and works directly with the subconscious patterns.

**Hypnosis creates lasting change.** Because it works at the level of the subconscious — where habits, beliefs, and deep emotional associations live — the changes created in hypnotherapy tend to persist beyond the session. This is in contrast to coaching approaches that engage the conscious mind: useful, but less likely to change the automatic patterns that govern performance.

**For singers specifically,** clinical hypnotherapy addresses:
- The beliefs about your voice formed in childhood and adolescence
- The physical tension patterns associated with performance anxiety
- The inner critic that undermines technical work
- The state of calm, focused presence that allows the voice to function at its best

This is not mystical. It is applied neuroscience. And it works.
