If you suffer from migraines, you'll know that the condition involves far more than a headache. The prodrome phase, the aura, the intense pain, the sensitivity to light and sound, the post-drome exhaustion — a full migraine episode can take days out of your life and is one of the most debilitating conditions I encounter in clinical practice.

What's less well known is that hypnotherapy has a substantial body of research behind it as a treatment for migraine, both in terms of prevention and in managing acute episodes.

**Why does it work?** Several mechanisms have been identified:

*Stress reduction.* Stress is one of the most common migraine triggers. Hypnotherapy's profound effect on the nervous system — reducing cortisol, promoting parasympathetic activity, and improving overall stress regulation — addresses this trigger directly.

*Vascular response.* Migraines involve changes in blood flow in the brain. Certain hypnotherapy techniques specifically target vasodilation and vasoconstriction, helping to interrupt the vascular cascade before it develops into a full episode.

*Pain modulation.* Hypnosis has well-documented analgesic effects. In hypnotic states, the subjective experience of pain can be significantly reduced — not by suppressing awareness, but by changing the relationship with the pain signal.

*Anticipatory anxiety.* Many migraine sufferers develop significant anxiety around the fear of the next episode. This anxiety itself can become a trigger, creating a self-perpetuating cycle. Hypnotherapy addresses this pattern directly.

If you've been managing migraines primarily with medication and haven't explored the psychological dimension, I'd encourage you to look into this. Not as a replacement for medical treatment — always work with your GP — but as a complementary approach that addresses mechanisms medication alone cannot reach.
