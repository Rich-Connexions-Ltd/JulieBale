There are approximately ten weeks between October and Christmas. For a significant proportion of the population, those ten weeks involve a familiar internal conversation — a negotiation between the part of you that wants to enjoy the season and the part of you that will be counting the cost in January.

"I'll be good until the work party." "I'll start properly in the new year." "One mince pie won't hurt — but then I need to be strict." "I've already ruined it so I might as well…"

If any of those thoughts sound familiar, you are not alone. You are also living in a pattern that is making Christmas significantly harder than it needs to be.

The food stress of the festive season is almost never actually about food. It's about control, self-worth, and the deeply embedded belief that enjoyment and health are in opposition — that you must choose between pleasure and propriety, and that you will be judged (by yourself and others) on which one you choose.

This year, I'd like to offer you a different frame.

What if the goal for Christmas wasn't to be "good"? What if it was simply to be *present* — to genuinely enjoy the meals, the events, the people, without the background noise of guilt and calculation?

This is not the same as abandoning all regard for your health. It's about releasing the *anxious relationship* with food that makes the season so exhausting — and replacing it with something much simpler: trust. In your body. In your choices. In your capacity to enjoy life without punishment.

If you'd like to do that work before Christmas arrives — ten weeks is exactly the right amount of time.
