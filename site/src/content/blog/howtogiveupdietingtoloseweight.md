This might be the most counterintuitive thing I'll ever say to you: if you want to lose weight, consider giving up dieting.

I understand if that lands with scepticism. We've been so thoroughly conditioned to believe that weight management requires dietary restriction that the idea of removing restriction feels like giving up. But I want to share what the research and my clinical experience actually show.

**Dieting increases food preoccupation.** When you restrict certain foods, you think about them more. This is a well-documented psychological effect — restriction creates obsession. The more "forbidden" a food becomes, the more mental space it occupies.

**Restriction damages your relationship with hunger.** Years of overriding natural hunger and fullness signals — eating when a diet plan says you should, not eating when you're hungry because it's "not time" — progressively erodes your ability to read your body's own signals.

**Restriction leads to the restrict-binge cycle.** The body, when chronically deprived, drives compensatory eating. This isn't weakness or failure. It's biology. Restriction creates the conditions for the very overeating it's trying to prevent.

**What works instead?** The approach I take in clinical practice is called intuitive eating with mindfulness. It involves:

- Relearning to read hunger and fullness signals
- Removing the moral charge from food choices ("good" and "bad" foods)
- Addressing the emotional triggers that drive non-hunger-based eating
- Building a genuinely positive relationship with your body and what it needs

This approach, supported by hypnotherapy, tends to produce slower change than a crash diet — but change that actually sticks. And, critically, it creates a quality of life during the process that restriction never does.

Giving up dieting doesn't mean giving up on your health. It means choosing a different — and far more effective — path to it.
