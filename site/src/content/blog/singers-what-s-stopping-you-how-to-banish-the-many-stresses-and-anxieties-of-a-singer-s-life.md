Being a singer is one of the most exposing things a person can choose to do. Your instrument is inside your body. Your voice is, in some fundamental way, *you*. When it fails to do what you need it to do — when the nerves kick in, when the note doesn't come, when the audition goes wrong — it can feel like a personal rejection rather than a technical problem.

Over thirty-five years of teaching, I have collected a fairly comprehensive list of the stresses and anxieties that affect singers. Let me address the most common ones.

**Performance anxiety.** The most universal and the most talked-about. The shaking voice, the racing heart, the blank mind in the moment you most need it. This is a physiological stress response — adrenaline doing what adrenaline does. The key is not to eliminate the response (it's actually fuel, when managed correctly) but to change your relationship with it.

**Vocal health anxiety.** The singer who wakes up every morning and immediately "tests" their voice with growing dread. Who cancels social events to protect their voice. Who lives in fear of losing the thing they love most. This anxiety, paradoxically, creates the tension that undermines the voice it's trying to protect.

**Comparison.** Social media has created a particularly virulent strain of this. The singer who spends twenty minutes watching other singers on Instagram and emerges feeling smaller than before. Comparison is not feedback. It is a thief.

**Imposter syndrome.** "I'm not a real singer." "I shouldn't be taking up a place on this course." "They'll soon realise I have no idea what I'm doing." This is extraordinarily common, even among highly accomplished performers.

The common thread through all of these is the mind — specifically, the beliefs and patterns that sit below conscious awareness and shape the experience of singing. This is exactly the territory that clinical hypnotherapy addresses. Not as a last resort, but as the most direct route to the singer you already have inside you.

What's stopping you? Let's find out.
