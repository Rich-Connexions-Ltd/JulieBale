I want to challenge something that I think has caused a great deal of unnecessary suffering. The idea that you cannot love your body until it looks a certain way.

This idea is so deeply embedded in our culture that many of us don't even question it. Of course you can't love your body as it is — that would mean accepting defeat, giving up on the goal of being thinner, healthier, more conventionally attractive. Wouldn't it?

No. And here is why: the war with your body is one of the primary causes of the problem it's supposedly trying to solve.

When you are at war with your body — when you view it as the enemy, as something to be controlled, disciplined, and punished — you create chronic stress. Chronic stress elevates cortisol. Elevated cortisol promotes fat storage, particularly around the abdomen. It also drives emotional eating, poor sleep, and decreased motivation. The war makes the problem worse.

Body acceptance — learning to regard your body with something approaching neutrality, even kindness — is not the same as resignation. It does not mean abandoning health goals. It means approaching those goals from a foundation of respect rather than contempt.

In clinical hypnotherapy, we work specifically with the image and the felt sense of the body — the deeply held beliefs and the emotional charge that surrounds them. This work creates the shift from adversarial to collaborative: your body as something you work *with*, rather than against.

It's a harder shift to make than most people expect. And it's far more transformational than any diet.
