There's a particular kind of performance I've watched hundreds of times. The singer walks onstage. They are technically capable — sometimes brilliantly so. But something is held back. There's a quality of apology in the performance, a quality of asking permission. *Is this okay? Am I too much? Am I taking up too much space?*

And the audience feels it. Not consciously — they couldn't tell you what's wrong. But they feel slightly less present, slightly less moved, than they could be.

This is not a technical problem. This is a power problem.

"Stepping into your power" has become something of a cliché in the personal development world, and I understand why people are sceptical of it. But when I use the phrase, I mean something very specific: the moment when a performer stops seeking permission from the audience and starts *offering* something to them instead.

The shift is subtle and total. The voice opens. The body expands. The eyes connect. The performance stops being about whether *you* are acceptable and starts being about what *you have to give*.

This is the state every singer I work with is ultimately aiming for. Not the perfect high note. Not the flawless technique. The honest, generous, powerful presence of a person who has decided they deserve to be heard.

Clinical hypnotherapy is one of the fastest ways I know to access this state — not by pumping you up with artificial confidence, but by removing the blocks that prevent you from accessing what's already there.

You have a voice. It deserves to be heard. Step into that.
