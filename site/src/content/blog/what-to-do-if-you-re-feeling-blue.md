January has a particular quality of blue. The tinsel is down, the days are short, the credit card statement has arrived. The resolution you made on New Year's Day feels further away than it did a week ago. The world has a slightly grey, slightly stuck quality that can be hard to shake.

First — this is normal. Not failure. Not weakness. Just the reality of being a feeling human in a cold, dark month.

Second — there are things that genuinely help. Not motivational quotes or silver linings. Actual, practical tools.

**Move.** I know this is the most obvious piece of advice, and I give it anyway because it works. Even fifteen minutes of walking changes the neurochemistry. Not enough to fix a serious depression — for that, please see your GP — but enough to break a spiral.

**Talk to your body, not your mind.** When we're feeling low, the instinct is to try to think our way through it. But the brain in distress doesn't think clearly. Breathing slowly and deliberately — breathing *out* longer than you breathe in — directly activates the parasympathetic nervous system and begins to calm the physiological state before we've worked anything out mentally.

**Notice what you're telling yourself.** The stories we run in difficult moments are rarely accurate. "Things will never get better." "I always do this." "What's the point." These are feelings dressed as facts. Clinical hypnotherapy is partly about learning to hear these stories without being governed by them.

**Reach out.** Even a small connection — a text, a walk with a friend, a conversation with a stranger in a coffee shop — interrupts the isolation that amplifies difficult feelings.

And finally: if the blue persists and deepens, please talk to someone. A GP, a therapist, a Samaritans volunteer (116 123 in the UK). Blue is not your permanent state. But sometimes you need support to find your way back.

Take care of yourself today.
