People sometimes ask me why the From First Note to Final Curtain programme ends with a live gala concert. Wouldn't it be simpler, they say, to just have regular lessons? Wouldn't the concert add unnecessary pressure?

I understand the question. But it reveals a fundamental misunderstanding of what the programme — and singing itself — is actually for.

The lessons build the voice. The technique sessions develop the instrument. The mindset and hypnotherapy work remove the blocks. But none of that means anything until the moment you stand in front of a real audience, in a real venue, and give your voice to the room.

The gala concert isn't the scary part of the programme. By the time we reach it, after six or twelve months of weekly 1:1 work, it is the *reward*. It is the moment of proof — the living, breathing evidence that everything you've built is real, that the transformation is genuine, and that you are exactly who you have been becoming all along.

I have stood in the wings of that concert more times than I can count. I have watched people walk onstage who, six months earlier, could barely sing a scale without apologising. I have seen them fill rooms with sound, with presence, with the particular joy of a person who has found something they thought was lost forever.

This is why the journey matters. Not the lessons. Not the technique. The destination — and the person you become on the way to it.

If you'd like to know what your first note might lead to, I'd love to find out with you.
