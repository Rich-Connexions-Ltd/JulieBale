Semaglutide — the active ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy — works by mimicking the GLP-1 hormone, which plays a key role in regulating appetite and blood sugar. In simple terms: it makes the brain think the body is full, significantly reducing the desire to eat.

A fascinating concept has emerged from the intersection of this pharmacology and clinical hypnotherapy: what if the brain could be trained, through hypnosis, to produce similar appetite-regulating effects?

This is not as far-fetched as it sounds. The brain is extraordinarily responsive to suggestion in hypnotic states. It cannot, as a general rule, distinguish between a vividly imagined experience and a real one — which is why the hypnotic gastric band works, why hypnoanalgesia reduces perceived pain, and why athletes who use mental rehearsal improve their physical performance.

"Hypnotic semaglutide" is still an emerging area, and the research is at an early stage. I want to be honest about that. We don't yet have the clinical trial data to make the claims that pharmaceutical companies make for the drug itself.

What we do have is substantial evidence that hypnotherapy can:
- Significantly reduce appetite and desire for specific foods
- Change the felt experience of hunger and fullness
- Address the emotional patterns that drive overeating beyond appetite

The crucial difference — and this is the answer to the yo-yo dieting question — is that hypnotherapy addresses the *psychological* factors that cause weight regain. The drug suppresses appetite while you take it. The therapy changes the underlying relationship with food, which continues to exert influence even after formal sessions end.

The end of yo-yo dieting isn't a drug. It's the permanent change in your relationship with food that only the mind can create. The question is whether you're ready to do that work.
