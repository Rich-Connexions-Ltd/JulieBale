When I was training as a clinical hypnotherapist, one of the things that struck me most was how many of the people I worked with struggled with the same fundamental challenge: they were far better at caring for others than they were at caring for themselves.

These weren't unkind people. They were often extraordinarily generous, supportive, compassionate — towards everyone but themselves. When something went wrong for someone they loved, they were all empathy. When something went wrong for themselves, they were all criticism.

This pattern has consequences. Not just emotionally, but physically, professionally, and creatively. The inner critic — the voice that says you're not good enough, not worthy, not deserving — is one of the most powerful suppressors of human potential I know.

In the context of singing, I see this play out in very concrete ways. Singers who struggle to sustain notes because they tense up at the most exposed moments. Singers whose voices shrink when they feel watched or judged. Singers who apologise before they've sung a note — as if their voice requires defending before it's even been heard.

Self-love, in this context, isn't about bubble baths and positive affirmations. It's about developing a genuine, active kindness towards yourself that allows you to take up space, make mistakes, and come back for more. It's about building the inner foundation that allows the outer voice to be truly free.

Hypnotherapy is one of the most effective tools I know for this work. Not because it makes you feel artificially good about yourself, but because it helps you access the deeper truth: that you were always worthy of the space your voice wants to take up.

That's not a luxury. That's the beginning of everything.
