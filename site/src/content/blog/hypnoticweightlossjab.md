The clinical hypnotherapy world has had its own version of an "appetite jab" for decades — long before semaglutide became a household name. It's called the hypnotic gastric band, and while it sounds dramatic, the underlying principle is elegantly simple.

In a hypnotic gastric band session, the therapist uses a deep hypnotic state to create a powerful, convincing experience of gastric band surgery — without any actual surgery. The subconscious mind, which cannot distinguish between a vividly imagined experience and a real one, processes this as if the band is physically in place.

The result, for many clients, is a genuine reduction in appetite, an increased sense of fullness after smaller portions, and a shift in eating behaviour that feels organic rather than effortful.

But here's where I want to offer a more nuanced view than the marketing often does. The hypnotic gastric band can be highly effective for reducing portion size and managing appetite — but it doesn't, by itself, address the emotional relationship with food. The eating that happens in response to stress, loneliness, boredom, or habit is not necessarily about hunger. And the gastric band, real or hypnotic, doesn't touch those patterns.

This is why my approach to weight management combines the direct appetite-regulation techniques of hypnosis with deeper work on the emotional and psychological patterns that drive overeating in the first place.

The "jab" is your mind. It is extraordinarily powerful. But it needs to be used thoughtfully — in combination with the deeper work that creates real, lasting change.
