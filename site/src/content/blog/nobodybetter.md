There's a phrase that comes back to me often. I can't remember exactly where I first heard it — perhaps it was a teacher, perhaps a book — but it's stayed with me for decades: *a dream that will need all the love you can give*.

I think about that a lot when I'm working with singers. Because almost every person who comes through my door is carrying a dream they've been protecting from the world for years. Sometimes for decades. A voice they've been keeping quiet. A performance they've been imagining but never quite allowing themselves to believe in.

The dream of singing is one of the most vulnerable kinds. Because your voice is *you*. Unlike a painting or a piece of writing, you can't put it down and walk away from it. If your voice trembles, you tremble. If your voice is rejected, it feels like *you* are rejected.

That vulnerability is why so many people never take the step. It's also why, when they do, it changes everything.

I'm here to tell you that the dream is worth it. Not in a motivational-poster kind of way — in a deeply practical, thirty-five-years-of-evidence kind of way. The singers I've watched find their voices have not just become better singers. They've become more themselves.

Your voice is your dream. It needs all the love you can give it. Including, perhaps, the love of a teacher who believes in it — and you — completely.

If you're ready to take that step, I'm here.
